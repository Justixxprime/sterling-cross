# Sterling & Cross — Every Photo & Video, Elite-Firm Spec (1 to 132)

You're not sourcing generic "law firm stock photos" — you're building the visual identity of a firm that reads as bigger and more important than it might currently be. Every top global firm (Kirkland & Ellis, Latham & Watkins, Clifford Chance, Wachtell) uses the same visual grammar: real named landmarks (not generic "a city"), architecture over people where possible, and restraint over polish. Below, every one of the 132 slots gets that treatment. Same order as before, nothing merged, nothing added or removed — just upgraded specificity.

---

### Homepage — 1–22

1. **Hero slide 1/4 — attorneys in conference.** Shot from a raised 3/4 angle down a long marble or dark-wood conference table, 5–6 figures, only the nearest 2 in focus (shallow depth of field), everyone mid-motion (turning a page, gesturing), nobody posed toward camera. Think the opening shot of a Wall Street Journal feature on a landmark deal.
2. **Hero slide 2/4 — global skyline.** Don't shoot "a skyline" — shoot **one unmistakable landmark silhouette** at blue hour: Chicago's river bend with the Willis Tower, or Manhattan's Financial District from the East River. Recognizability is the entire point — it should be identifiable in under a second.
3. **Hero slide 3/4 — boardroom negotiation.** Tighter, 2–3 people, side-lit from a single window (hard directional light, not flat fill), hands and an open folio in frame, faces slightly turned from camera. This is your "$2B deal closing at midnight" beat.
4. **Hero slide 4/4 — second skyline.** A different landmark from #2 for contrast — Canary Wharf/The Gherkin for London, or Marina Bay Sands for Singapore, whichever matches a real or aspirational office city.
5. **Small decorative graphic (no alt text).** Skip photography entirely here — a subtle, single-color line-art motif (the scales, an architectural cornice detail) works better than a literal photo and reads as more custom/expensive.
6. **80×80 client avatar.** A real client (with consent) or a dignified stand-in — three-quarter angle, neutral dark backdrop, no visible logo or branded clothing.
7. 🎥 **"Firm overview film" poster.** The best frame from a 60–90s film that should open on your flagship lobby or a drone descent onto your HQ skyline, cut to 3–4 partners speaking 1 sentence each on camera, close on your wordmark. Poster frame = the skyline or lobby shot, not a mid-sentence talking head.
8. **Decorative attorney portrait.** Reuse one of your real 14 headshots once shot — don't source a separate "generic" one; consistency compounds trust.
9. **Decorative office interior.** Reuse a real interior from the Locations set (#79–92) rather than a separate stock shot.
10. **Senior/founding partner portrait (700×900, tall).** This is your most consequential single photograph. Formal three-quarter length, arms uncrossed, standing near a window or bookshelf, natural authority rather than a stiff corporate pose — this is the "Chambers Global Band 1" photo.
11. **.** A trading floor mid-session, motion-blurred in the background, one sharp foreground element (a terminal, a hand on a phone).
12. **Litigation & arbitration.** An empty, grand courtroom or federal courthouse rotunda — scale and gravity, no people needed.
13. **Private equity.** A closing-table moment: pens on a signature page, out-of-focus hands, warm desk-lamp light.
14. **Restructuring.** Similar financial register to #13 but cooler, more clinical light — this practice is about control amid distress, the lighting should feel tenser.
15. **Financial services (industry).** A bank's actual trading floor or a fintech's glass-walled office — never a teller counter, that reads small.
16. **Technology.** A server room's blue-lit rack corridor, or a clean product-lab bench shot — avoid "people around a laptop."
17. **Energy & infrastructure.** A wind farm at golden hour, or a substation's dramatic industrial geometry.
18. **Healthcare.** A hospital atrium or a research lab bench, bright but not sterile-cold; avoid stock "doctor smiling at camera."
19. **"Cross-border merger" case study.** Two skylines double-exposed or split-framed, or a single signing-table shot with two flags/passports subtly visible.
20. **"Restructuring" case study.** A single dramatic architectural shot — a building facade in hard shadow works better than another boardroom.
21. **"Litigation" case study.** Courthouse steps, wide angle, dawn or dusk light for gravity.
22. **"Capital markets/IPO" case study.** A stock exchange floor or an opening-bell-style shot — energy and scale.

---

**The one thing that separates "biggest firm in the world" from "trying to look like one":** consistency, not individual photo quality. The 14 attorney headshots being shot in the exact same session with the exact same setup will do more for the "important, established, global" read than any single hero image, however good. If you can only fund one real shoot, that's the one.
