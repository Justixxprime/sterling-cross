Legal Glossary

Legal terms, in plain English.

No jargon left unexplained. A running glossary of the terms that come up most across our practice areas.

A B C D E F G H I J L M N P Q R S T V W

A

Arbitration

A private dispute resolution process where a neutral third party (the arbitrator) hears both sides and issues a binding decision, typically faster and more confidential than litigation.

Asset Purchase Agreement

A contract governing the sale of a company's specific assets, rather than its shares, often used to let a buyer avoid assuming the seller's liabilities.

Amicus Brief

A legal document filed by someone not party to a case, offering the court additional information or a perspective relevant to the outcome.

B

Beneficiary

A person or entity named to receive assets from a trust, will, life insurance policy, or retirement account.

Breach of Contract

A failure, without legal excuse, to perform any promise that forms all or part of a contract.

C

Chapter 11

A section of the U.S. Bankruptcy Code allowing a business to reorganize its debts and continue operating, rather than liquidating.

Class Action

A lawsuit where one or several plaintiffs sue on behalf of a larger group of people with the same or similar claims against the defendant.

Custody, Legal

The right to make major decisions about a child's upbringing, including education, healthcare, and religion, which can be held solely or jointly.

Custody, Physical

Determines where a child primarily lives and which parent is responsible for day-to-day care.

D

Discovery

The pre-trial phase in a lawsuit where each party can obtain evidence from the other through depositions, document requests, and interrogatories.

Due Diligence

The investigation a buyer conducts before a transaction to verify a target company's financials, contracts, and liabilities.

Durable Power of Attorney

A legal document authorizing someone to act on your behalf in financial or legal matters, which remains in effect even if you become incapacitated.

E

Earnout

A contractual provision in an acquisition where the seller receives additional payment if the business hits specific performance targets after closing.

Escrow

Funds or assets held by a neutral third party until specific contractual conditions are met, common in real estate and M&A closings.

Executor

The person named in a will to carry out its instructions, including distributing assets and settling the deceased's debts.

F

Force Majeure

A contract clause that excuses a party from performance when extraordinary events, like natural disasters or war, make performance impossible.

Fiduciary Duty

A legal obligation to act in the best interest of another party, common among corporate directors, trustees, and attorneys.

G

Grand Jury

A group of citizens convened to determine whether there is enough evidence to formally charge someone with a crime.

Guardian ad Litem

A person appointed by a court to represent the best interests of a child or incapacitated person during legal proceedings.

H

H-1B Visa

A U.S. work visa allowing employers to temporarily hire foreign workers in specialty occupations requiring a bachelor's degree or higher.

I

Indemnification

A contractual promise by one party to compensate another for specific losses or damages, commonly negotiated in M&A and commercial agreements.

Injunction

A court order requiring a party to do, or refrain from doing, a specific act, often used to prevent irreparable harm before a case is fully resolved.

Intestate

Dying without a valid will, in which case state law determines how the deceased's assets are distributed.

J

Joint Venture

A business arrangement where two or more parties combine resources to pursue a specific project while remaining independent entities.

L

LOI (Letter of Intent)

A preliminary, often non-binding document outlining the key terms of a proposed deal before a definitive agreement is drafted.

Living Trust

A legal arrangement created during a person's lifetime to hold assets, which can help beneficiaries avoid the probate process.

M

Mediation

A voluntary process where a neutral third party helps disputing parties reach a mutually acceptable resolution, without imposing a binding decision.

Merger

A transaction combining two companies into one, as distinguished from an acquisition where one company purchases another outright.

N

NDA (Non-Disclosure Agreement)

A contract obligating one or more parties to keep specified information confidential, commonly used before sharing sensitive business information.

NIL (Name, Image & Likeness)

The legal right of athletes, particularly collegiate athletes, to profit from the commercial use of their identity.

Negligence

A legal claim requiring proof of four elements: that the defendant owed a duty of care, breached it, the breach caused harm, and the harm produced compensable damages. Forms the basis of most personal injury and wrongful death claims.

P

Power of Attorney

A legal document granting someone the authority to act on another person's behalf in specified matters.

Probate

The court-supervised process of validating a will, paying a deceased person's debts, and distributing remaining assets to heirs.

Prenuptial Agreement

A contract entered into before marriage that outlines how assets and debts will be divided in the event of divorce or death.

Q

QDRO (Qualified Domestic Relations Order)

A court order that divides retirement plan benefits between divorcing spouses without triggering early withdrawal penalties.

R

Recapitalization

A restructuring of a company's debt and equity mix, often used to stabilize finances or facilitate a change in ownership.

Regulation D

An SEC exemption allowing companies to raise capital through private securities offerings without a full public registration.

S

Severance Agreement

A contract between an employer and a departing employee, typically providing compensation in exchange for a release of legal claims.

Subpoena

A legal document compelling a person to testify or produce documents in a legal proceeding.

SSDI (Social Security Disability Insurance)

A federal disability benefit based on a claimant's work history and payroll tax contributions, administered by the Social Security Administration.

SSI (Supplemental Security Income)

A needs-based federal disability benefit for people with limited income and resources, regardless of work history.

Statute of Limitations

The deadline within which a lawsuit must be filed. Varies by state and claim type, and missing it generally bars the claim permanently.

Survival Action

A claim brought by an estate for damages the deceased could have recovered had they survived, separate from and alongside a wrongful death claim.

T

Tortious Interference

A legal claim arising when a third party intentionally damages an existing contract or business relationship between two other parties.

Trustee

A person or institution holding and managing assets in a trust for the benefit of named beneficiaries.

V

Vesting

The process by which an employee earns non-forfeitable rights to employer-provided benefits, such as stock options, over time.

VA Disability Rating

A percentage assigned by the Department of Veterans Affairs reflecting the severity of a service-connected disability, which determines monthly compensation.

W

Whistleblower

An individual who reports illegal or unethical conduct within an organization, often protected from retaliation under specific statutes.

Work Product Doctrine

A legal protection shielding materials an attorney prepares in anticipation of litigation from being obtained by the opposing party.

Wrongful Death

A civil claim brought by surviving family members when a death is caused by another party's negligence or wrongful act, compensating losses like lost support and loss of companionship.
